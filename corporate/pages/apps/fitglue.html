FitGlue.

Fitness automation that joins up the apps you already use. Record a workout once; FitGlue fills in the rest and sends it where it belongs.

What it does

  • Collects a workout from where you recorded it, such as a strength session in Hevy or a run on Strava.
  • Enriches it: heart rate and zones from your watch, effort and training load, calories, personal records, weather and location, your photos, and a short written summary.
  • Delivers the finished activity to the places you choose, such as Strava or a public activity page.
  • Rounds up each week, month and year, and shows your sleep, weight, activity and nutrition trends alongside your training.

How FitGlue uses Google Health

When you connect Google Health, FitGlue asks for read-only access to your health metrics (heart rate, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, weight), activity and fitness, sleep, and nutrition. It uses them to add heart rate to the workouts you record, and to show you your own sleep, body and nutrition trends. It never writes to Google Health, never sells the data, and never uses it for advertising or to train AI models.

Full details are in the privacy policy. You can remove access at any time from your Google Account.

Retired

FitGlue was retired as a public product in 2026. It is no longer open for sign-ups and the public service has closed; it still runs privately for its maker, which is why it keeps a Google Health connection. For anything about it, including deleting your data, email hello@ripixel.co.uk.
